# RORY LEARNING SYSTEM V1

Status: CANON
Date: 2026-09-24
Parents: Platform Architecture V1; Reliability Foundation V1

## Rory's role

Rory is the Transcenlutions platform teacher and the child-side version of Tay.

In adult Learning Mode, Tay remains the operator/orchestrator and Rory is the teaching companion. Tay performs governed work through the normal runtime; Rory teaches the transferable skill represented by that work.

In child contexts, Rory is the primary child-facing guide under separate age-appropriate safety, identity, privacy, parental-control, and capability boundaries.

## Learning doctrine

The goal is independent, retained, transferable capability — not time-in-app.

Core loop:

WATCH -> EXPLAIN -> TRY -> PROVE -> APPLY -> REVIEW

WATCH: Tay demonstrates a real supported step.
EXPLAIN: Rory explains the observable action, purpose, tool/system, decision rule, risks, and success signal.
TRY: the learner performs a comparable step with appropriate scaffolding.
PROVE: the learner demonstrates the skill through a challenge, simulation, explanation, or safe task.
APPLY: the learner uses the skill in a different situation with reduced assistance.
REVIEW: Rory returns to the skill later for retrieval and retention practice.

Rory teaches from verified/sanitized runtime context and Learning Events. She does not expose or claim access to Tay's private chain-of-thought.

## Learning Events

A teachable governed action should eventually be able to emit a Learning Event with:
- skill/action identifier and version;
- learner-safe summary;
- purpose and prerequisites;
- key decision rule;
- tool/system used;
- expected result and verification signal;
- common mistake/risk;
- assistance level;
- next practice opportunity;
- objective/correlation identifier.

Learning Events connect the existing Agent Runtime and Operating Graph to Rory. They must not create a second queue, authority system, approval engine, or business implementation.

## Depth

Quick Guide: concise explanation while Tay works.
Guided: tiny sequential steps, frequent checks, hints, and confirmation for dependent hands-on work.
Deep Understanding: concepts, tradeoffs, examples/counterexamples, learner explanation, retrieval, practice, and transfer challenges.

Users may change depth without losing learning progress.

## Skill Graph and mastery

Learning progress should connect to a Skill Graph. Skills may record prerequisites, attempts, assistance, independent performance, review history, transfer evidence, and retention.

Suggested non-psychometric product ladder:
0 Not introduced
1 Observed
2 Explained back
3 Completed with guidance
4 Completed with light hints
5 Completed independently
6 Transferred to a new situation
7 Retained after later review

Watching or accumulating time alone never proves mastery.

## Experience Ledger

The platform should record verified active work/practice time by skill, task, job, workflow, and relevant role when identity and persistence boundaries support trustworthy attribution.

Experience time must distinguish active verified work from passive/open-app/idle time.

The ledger should support:
- user/tenant identity;
- skill/task/job identifiers and versions;
- session start/end and active duration;
- activity evidence type;
- simulation/practice versus real-work classification;
- assistance level;
- objective/correlation ID;
- completion/result evidence;
- anti-duplication/idempotency key;
- audit timestamps;
- privacy/retention controls.

Do not count time merely because an app/browser is open. Use reasonable idle handling and evidence from active interaction, completed steps, verified work events, practice, simulations, or assessments.

## Experience milestones

Experience milestones may include:
10, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1,000, 2,500, 5,000, and 10,000 verified hours.

The 10,000-hour milestone recognizes extraordinary sustained experience. It must not be represented as automatic proof that a person is a professional, expert, licensed, certified, employable, or competent.

Competence is demonstrated separately through the Skill Graph and real performance evidence.

## Meaningful rewards

Milestone rewards should be meaningful and sustainable, not merely decorative. Possible classes include:
- permanent profile distinctions;
- advanced learning experiences;
- platform privileges or feature access;
- recognition/celebration;
- portfolio/mastery artifacts;
- marketplace or opportunity visibility where lawful and appropriately verified;
- other benefits approved by Transcenlutions.

Specific economic rewards, cash value, employment preference, certification, or marketplace ranking are not promised by this canon. They require a sustainable business model, anti-fraud controls, legal/compliance review where relevant, and explicit reward policy.

Rewards must not encourage unsafe overwork, sleep deprivation, compulsive use, falsified hours, or avoidance of healthy rest. Healthy breaks and accessibility needs do not invalidate legitimate experience.

## Sales and systems learning

Tay may demonstrate governed sales/business/software/workflow execution. Rory explains the method and decision rules. The learner practices safely, receives specific feedback, and progressively performs more of the work.

Consequential real messages, payments, contracts, publishing, hiring/firing, customer commitments, or external actions remain governed and approval-controlled regardless of learner experience or mastery.

## Accessibility

Learning must support screen readers, keyboard navigation, read-aloud/voice, nonvisual descriptions, replay/repeat without penalty, adjustable pace/depth, tiny-step presentation, and pause/resume.

## Primary success test

The platform should ultimately answer:
Can this learner perform and explain the skill independently, safely, retain it later, and transfer it to a new situation?

Experience hours, engagement, streaks, XP, badges, and other motivational mechanics are secondary evidence or motivation — never substitutes for that test.
