# Transcenlutions revenue path

## The first paid problem

Service businesses lose qualified leads and recurring contracts between the first inquiry, the quote, and follow-up. Start with commercial cleaning and similar local recurring-service operators.

## Offer now: Tay Revenue Recovery Pilot

Sell a 14-day, founder-led service with a narrow promise:

- map the customer's current inquiry and quote process;
- capture open opportunities from the sources the customer approves;
- draft reviewed replies and follow-ups;
- organize walkthroughs or calls;
- report response time, quotes followed up, booked work, and pipeline value.

The customer approves every outbound message. No guaranteed revenue, automatic booking, unattended calling, or full CRM replacement is included.

Test pricing as a setup fee plus a monthly service fee. Do not publish a permanent price until three pilots reveal labor, tooling, and outcome data.

## Revenue gate

Do not build the broad platform until we have:

1. Ten owner interviews in one segment.
2. Three real quote pipelines reviewed with permission.
3. One paid pilot.
4. One documented before-and-after workflow result.
5. A repeatable delivery checklist that another trained operator can follow.

## Platform direction after proof

Once the pilot works, extract the reusable pieces into Tay Core:

IDENTITY → AUTHORIZATION → OPERATING GRAPH → AGENTS → SKILLS → MEMORY → FILES → INTEGRATIONS → AUDIT → BILLING

Then add user-created agents with explicit plan limits, permissions, logs, and spend controls.

## Crowne Shards and living agents

Treat Crowne Shards as a later usage and compute-credit model. First define what a shard pays for, how it expires, what keeps an agent active, and how users see usage. Never sell “alive” or always-on agents until uptime, cost, shutdown, privacy, and recovery behavior are tested.

## Islands and Polsia direction

Use the user's Islands and Polsia concepts as a shared-world/product layer above Tay Core. Do not merge codebases or build a virtual economy during the first revenue pilot. Capture domain ideas in Venture Foundry and promote them only when they have customer evidence, a responsible owner, and a revenue hypothesis.

## Tay building Tay

Tay may propose and implement small, reviewed improvements to itself through the coding tools. Self-modification remains gated by Git checkpoints, tests, an explicit file scope, and user review. Tay does not autonomously rewrite its own permissions, billing, identity, or safety rules.
